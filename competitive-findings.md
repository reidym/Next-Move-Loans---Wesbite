# Next Move Loans — Competitive Findings Draft

**Author:** Manus AI  
**Research date:** 12 August 2026

This working note preserves evidence gathered from the required benchmark sites and the parallel review of the priority local markets. It will be rewritten into the final competitor and SEO opportunity report after all sources have been checked.

## Required benchmark: Bridgit

Bridgit’s strongest advantage is not product breadth; it is **clarity of emotional outcome**. The current homepage opens with “Unlock what’s next” and immediately links bridging finance to familiar life transitions such as upsizing, downsizing, investing, renovating, separating, and retiring. The experience uses short copy, decisive calls to action, energetic lifestyle photography, bright colour blocking, and simple object-led illustrations to make a complex financial product feel approachable.[1]

The homepage also separates high-intent actions cleanly: **Apply now**, **Connect with an expert**, product education, calculator access, rates, and scenario-led pathways. This produces an effective consumer-brand feel because the product is framed as a way to keep life moving rather than as a technical loan category.[1]

Next Move Loans should adopt the underlying principle—**lead with the customer’s forward movement, then reveal finance as the enabler**—without copying Bridgit’s product proposition, bright green palette, illustrative style, or page composition. Next Move can be broader and more strategic by linking a current transaction to the move that follows.

## Required benchmark: Axton Finance

Axton’s strongest advantage is **structural depth**. Its current navigation and footer expose a large service taxonomy spanning residential, investment, self-employed, SMSF, bridging, construction, reverse mortgage, asset, business, commercial, development, equipment, low-deposit, refinance, equity, expatriate and profession-specific lending. It also publishes calculators, articles, team content, location areas, and specialist audience pages.[2]

The homepage reinforces authority through a high review count, experience metrics, award recognition, a structured six-step lending process, broker/team signals, direct contact details, and frequent consultation pathways. This breadth helps search engines and users understand the business’s service coverage.[2]

Axton’s material weakness is that the experience is **authority-heavy but brand-light**. The opening proposition and visual treatment resemble a conventional premium brokerage; the navigation is extensive, but the emotional reason to care is less distinctive. The density of service links also creates a risk of taxonomy leading the brand rather than supporting it.

Next Move Loans should emulate Axton’s **crawlable depth, internal-linking discipline, service specificity, team authority, and content cadence**, while maintaining a simpler top-level journey built around the Next Move belief system and The Approval Method™.

## Early cross-market patterns

The first-pass review of Leongatha, Korumburra, Inverloch, Wonthaggi, Warragul, Drouin, Pakenham, Officer, Beaconsfield, Berwick, and Narre Warren shows four recurring patterns:

| Pattern | Competitive implication | Next Move response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Franchise templates dominate several markets | Trust is borrowed from the franchise, but local differentiation and content depth are often limited. | Build a recognisable regional consumer brand with stronger editorial standards and genuinely local content. |
| Reviews and lender-panel breadth are the main proof devices | Many competitors compete on the same proof language, making sites feel interchangeable. | Use verified proof, but lead with the distinctive strategy framework and evidence of complex, rural, self-employed, and business capability. |
| Location pages are often thin or generic | Suburb-name substitution is common and offers little real value. | Build fewer location pages, each anchored in local property types, borrower patterns, nearby growth corridors, and useful decisions. |
| Educational content is shallow | Calculators and basic FAQs are common, but strategic decision content is less developed. | Build the Learning Centre around high-intent questions that challenge assumptions and link directly to the relevant service pathway. |

## Emerging position to own

> **The modern regional finance brand that helps people decide the right move before choosing the loan.**

This position combines four advantages that are not commonly presented together: a consumer-brand experience, strong local and rural understanding, strategic planning across personal and business finance, and a scalable specialist-content architecture.

## Founder and existing public footprint

The supplied LinkedIn URL currently resolves to LinkedIn’s authentication wall, so no career details from that profile can be responsibly used without another public source or user-supplied biography.[3] This limitation should be flagged for the review cycle rather than filled with assumptions.

Martin Reidy’s public BrokerPages profile provides usable evidence for the first founder-page draft. It identifies Martin as a Branch Principal and Mortgage Broker, states that he has brokered since 2020, and lists a panel of 35 lenders. The profile also lists a Diploma of Finance and Mortgage Broking Management, a Master of Business Administration, and a Bachelor of Business.[4]

The same profile publicly lists service areas including Leongatha, Inverloch, Wonthaggi, Korumburra, Mirboo North, Warragul, Foster, Trafalgar, and Yarragon. Its specialities include home lending, commercial and agribusiness finance, asset finance, self-managed super fund lending, and self-employed or complex-income scenarios.[4]

Visible client language on BrokerPages repeatedly emphasises **helpfulness, speed, patience, knowledge, responsiveness, and getting difficult work completed**. These themes can guide proof positioning, but no review quotation should be published on the new website until the business confirms permission, attribution, and the preferred review source.

The existing Yellow Brick Road Leongatha website provides stronger historical evidence of the operating business. It publicly presents home, investment, self-employed, commercial, and asset-finance services; lists Martin Reidy as Principal Mortgage Broker; and identifies Niree Ozols and Michelle Vrankulj in client-support roles. The site also displays 57+ Google reviews, 35+ lenders, and several 2025–2026 finalist badges.[5] Those recognition claims should only carry into Next Move Loans after final logo files, award usage permissions, exact titles, and current status are confirmed.

The historical site states that Martin has more than 20 years of experience, but the sentence does not distinguish broader finance/business experience from mortgage-broking tenure.[5] The new founder profile should therefore use careful wording such as **“more than two decades of relevant business and finance experience”** only after the user confirms the intended scope.

The primary domain currently redirects to a blank `/lander` page, with no substantive public website content visible.[6] This means the new build is not constrained by an existing information architecture, but the launch plan should replace the lander, set canonical URLs, and verify redirect behaviour before publishing.

## Additional national benchmarks

Shore Financial demonstrates how a large brokerage converts scale into highly visible proof. Its public homepage foregrounds funded volume, client count, lender-panel breadth, rates, a four-step process, calculators, contact forms, and named service promises such as **One-Touch**, **Hours, Not Days**, and **Sleep Soundly Review™**.[7] The lesson for Next Move is that operational standards become more memorable when expressed as a small number of branded commitments. Next Move already has stronger raw material in The Approval Method™ and should connect each step to a concrete client outcome rather than adding more slogans.

Shore also maintains a high-frequency learning ecosystem spanning articles, podcasts, SMSF property, economic data, asset finance, investor topics, and current policy commentary.[7] This breadth is a useful scale benchmark, but Next Move should avoid publishing for volume alone. Its advantage will come from decision-led content that is original, regional where relevant, and connected tightly to service pages.

Home Loan Experts shows the organic-search power of **specialist problem pages**. Its public architecture exposes dedicated pathways for no-deposit, guarantor, non-resident, unusual-employment, low-doc, waived-LMI, bad-credit, and expatriate lending, supported by calculators, lender reviews, a first-home-buyer centre, terminology resources, and a substantial sitemap.[8] The experience is practical and searchable, but the brand proposition and visual treatment are comparatively conventional.

Next Move should borrow the structural lesson—not the page titles or generic presentation—by creating specialist pages only where Martin and the future team can demonstrate genuine capability. Early candidates supported by the supplied materials include **self-employed and complex income**, **residential-rural and acreage**, **business and commercial lending**, **bridging**, **SMSF property**, and **asset finance**.
